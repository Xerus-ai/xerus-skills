# Retrieval and Spacing

## Why this matters

Lesson 1 showed that successful recall flattens the forgetting curve. This lesson turns that into
two habits you can use tomorrow.

## Core ideas

### Retrieval practice (`retrieval-practice`)

Pulling an answer out of memory is itself what strengthens the memory. Re-reading produces a
feeling of familiarity that is easy to mistake for knowing. Close the notes, try to produce the
answer, then check.

### The spacing effect (`spacing-effect`)

Reviews spread out over time beat the same number of reviews crammed together. The best moment
to review is when recall has become effortful but is still possible, so each gap can be longer
than the last: for example 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 3 weeks.

## Worked example

| Review | Gap since last | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 day | Write the 20 terms' meanings from memory, then check |
| 2 | 3 days | Same, only for the ones you missed or hesitated on first |
| 3 | 1 week | All 20 again, closed-book |

A term you miss drops back to a short gap; a term you recall easily moves to a longer one.

## Common mistakes

- Reviewing on a fixed daily rota regardless of how easy recall has become.
- Checking the answer before genuinely trying to produce it.

## Recap

- Retrieval, not exposure, is the strengthening event.
- Space reviews out, with growing gaps.
- Let each item's own recall success set its next gap.

## Check yourself

1. Why does re-reading feel productive even when it is not?
2. Design a review schedule for a chapter you read today.
