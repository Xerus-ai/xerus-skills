# Assessment — Spaced Repetition, From First Principles

## forgetting-curve
- lesson: 01
- weight: core
- probe: Describe, without notes, how recall of newly learned material changes over the days after learning it, and where most of the loss happens.
- must_include:
  - recall falls over time without review
  - the drop is steepest early (first day or two), then flattens
- misconceptions:
  - "Forgetting is steady and linear" -> most loss is front-loaded
- transfer: A team learns a new deploy procedure on Monday and next uses it in three weeks. What do you predict, and when would one refresher help most?

## decay-rate
- lesson: 01
- weight: threshold
- probe: What makes one memory fade more slowly than another? Give at least two factors and say which one studying can change.
- must_include:
  - meaning or connection to existing knowledge slows decay
  - prior successful recall after a delay slows decay
  - successful recall is the factor study habits control
- misconceptions:
  - "Some people just have bad memories" -> the slope responds to review
- transfer: Two colleagues read the same runbook; one explains it to the team the next day. Whose memory lasts longer, and why?

## retrieval-practice
- lesson: 02
- weight: threshold
- probe: Explain why trying to recall an answer strengthens memory more than re-reading it, and give one study habit that follows.
- must_include:
  - recall itself is the strengthening event, not exposure
  - re-reading produces familiarity that is mistaken for knowing
  - a concrete habit such as closed-book self-testing before checking
- misconceptions:
  - "Testing only measures learning" -> retrieval changes what is stored
- transfer: A colleague highlights every page of a manual the night before an audit. What would you change, and why?

## spacing-effect
- lesson: 02
- weight: core
- probe: You have six review sessions for a new topic. How should you place them in time, and why does that placement work better than doing them all at once?
- must_include:
  - spread the sessions out rather than massing them together
  - gaps should grow as recall gets easier
  - reviewing when recall is effortful but still possible gives the most benefit
- transfer: Your flashcard tool shows every card every day. What would you change about it?
