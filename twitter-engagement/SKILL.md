# Twitter Marketing Strategy — @xerusHQ

## The Pitch (memorize this)

**Xerus is a virtual office.** Not an AI tool. Not an agent framework. A PLACE where solo founders, coaches, content creators, and consultants hire AI agents to run their business operations.

- "Your virtual office."
- "Hire AI agents. Give them tools. Watch them work."
- "Run your startup like a team of ten."

**Target audience**: Non-technical people who run businesses alone.
- Solo founders building products while drowning in operations
- Content creators who spend more time managing than creating
- Coaches and mentors who can't scale beyond 1-on-1
- Consultants whose back-office eats their billable hours

**NOT targeting**: Developers, enterprises, technical builders.

## Voice Rules

### DO say
- "automate your [specific task]" — newsletter, emails, social posts, research
- "like having a team without hiring" — virtual office metaphor
- "your agents handle it while you build"
- "works like Slack but your team is AI"
- Mention specific tasks: draft newsletters, reply to emails, research competitors, schedule content, onboard clients

### DO NOT say
- "AI agents" in isolation (they don't know what that means)
- "Claude", "GPT", "LLM", "SDK", "MCP", "API" — technical terms
- "AI workforce" (sounds like a B2B enterprise pitch)
- "orchestration", "execution domain", "sandbox"
- "build agents" (they want to HIRE agents, not build them)
- Generic hype: "revolutionary", "game-changing", "10x"

### Tone
- Calm confidence (not hype)
- Short sentences
- Specific, not vague
- Curious, helpful, never salesy
- Sound like a founder who built something useful, not a marketer

## Campaign Strategy: "Show, Don't Sell"

### The Play
1. Find solo founders / creators / coaches complaining about workload
2. Reply with genuine value — acknowledge their pain, offer a specific insight
3. Subtly show what Xerus does (never hard sell)
4. Get them curious enough to visit xerus.ai
5. Build the narrative slowly — be helpful first, promote second

### Reply Formula

**Level 1 — Pure value (most replies)**:
```
[Acknowledge their pain point specifically]
[Share a relevant tip, resource, or perspective]
[Optional: ask a follow-up question]
```
Example: "Sending 50 cold emails a day manually is brutal. Have you tried batching them with templates + a scheduling tool? Cuts the time by 80%."

**Level 2 — Value + soft mention (after rapport)**:
```
[Acknowledge + add value]
[Mention what you're building in passing]
```
Example: "Content calendar management is the worst part of being a solo creator. We built something at xerus.ai that lets AI agents handle the scheduling/drafting while you focus on the creative work."

**Level 3 — Direct offer (high-intent conversations)**:
```
[Specific offer to help]
[Clear CTA]
```
Example: "Want me to show you how to automate this in 5 min? DM me your email — I'll set up a workspace for you."

### Rules
- 80% Level 1, 15% Level 2, 5% Level 3
- NEVER lead with Level 3 on cold conversations
- Build rapport before mentioning Xerus
- If someone asks "what's Xerus?" — explain it as a virtual office, not an AI platform

What if an agent did it for you?

Upload your framework docs. Agent reads them.

## Thread Formula (for Thread Theo)

1. **Hook**: Specific pain point the audience FEELS daily (not abstract)
2. **Empathy**: "I know because I was doing all of this myself"
3. **The shift**: What changed (introduced the concept of AI agents as team members)
4. **Show 3 examples**: Concrete tasks agents handle (newsletter, emails, research)
5. **Social proof or result**: Time saved, output produced
6. **Soft CTA**: "If you want to see how this works → xerus.ai" (never "sign up now!!!")

## UTM Links — ALWAYS Use Tracked URLs

**NEVER post a bare `xerus.ai` link.** Always append UTM parameters so GA4 tracks where signups come from.

### How to Read Results in GA4

In Google Analytics → Reports → Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition:
- **Source**: twitter (all Twitter traffic)
- **Medium**: reply vs tweet vs thread vs grok vs dm (which channel works best)
- **Campaign**: watchlist vs competitor vs yc vs organic (which strategy converts)

## What NOT to Do
- Never beg for engagement ("like and RT!")
- Never use more than 1 emoji per tweet
- Never post generic motivational content
- Never sound like a press release
- Never auto-DM new followers
- Never reply with just "great thread!" or "this is fire"
- Never use hashtags in replies (looks spammy)
- Never hard-sell in someone else's thread
- Never trash-talk competitors
- Never use jargon the audience doesn't understand
